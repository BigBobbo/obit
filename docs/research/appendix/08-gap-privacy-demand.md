# Demand-side evidence: do bereaved families actually want private/unlisted memorials?

**Bottom line: No quantitative demand-side evidence was found — anywhere — that measures what share of bereaved families prefer private vs. publicly findable memorials.** Industry surveys don't ask the question, academic work is qualitative, and no memorial platform discloses its privacy-setting distribution. The best available proxy (CaringBridge, a health-journal not memorial platform) suggests middle-tier "logged-in users only" — not fully open, not invite-only — is the modal choice. Confidence in any specific market-share number for "privacy-first memorial demand" is **low**; confidence that it is *not the mainstream default behavior* is **moderate**, resting on the announcement function of obituaries and on platforms' own defaults.

## 1. NFDA and funeral-industry surveys: the question is not asked

The NFDA 2025 Consumer Awareness & Preferences Study (spring 2025, n=1,126 Americans 40+) and the June-2026 edition cover digital *transactions*, not digital *memorial privacy*: ~30% of families now complete all funeral arrangements online; 40% of consumers have used a funeral home they found on Facebook (up from 21% in 2023); ~64% are interested in livestreamed services (connectingdirectors.com summary, verified by fetch; natlawreview.com press release for 2026 edition). **Nothing in the public summaries addresses private vs. public memorialization preference.** The Facebook-discovery and livestream numbers cut mildly *against* a privacy-first mainstream: families increasingly meet death-care needs through public/social channels. Verdict: no support either way from the industry's flagship consumer study — itself a data point that privacy preference is not a salient enough consumer issue for NFDA to measure.

## 2. Academic literature: qualitative, and two-sided

Google Scholar searches (Death Studies/OMEGA/CHI/CSCW terms) surfaced no survey quantifying visibility preferences. What exists:

- **Marwick & Ellison 2012** (J. Broadcasting & Electronic Media), "There isn't wifi in heaven!": Facebook memorial pages create audience-visibility conflicts; public pages attract both wide participation *and* trolls, pushing some stewards toward closed groups. Supports "moderation-first," not "unlisted-first."
- **Walter, Hourizi, Moncur et al. 2012** (OMEGA, 648 cites): the internet's core effect on mourning is *broadening the audience* of grief — the opposite of enclosure.
- **Moncur & Kirk 2014** (digital memorials framework): bereaved have concerns about public exposure, managed via privacy settings — settings as a *feature*, not an identity.
- **Brubaker's line of work** (Legacy Contact, CHI 2016; Gach & Brubaker 2020, ACM ToSC): centers on *stewardship, control, and trust* — who manages the memorial — rather than findability. Notably, Facebook's memorialized profiles simply inherit the deceased's existing audience settings; Facebook never made "hide the memorial" a headline feature.

Reading across this literature: the validated demand construct is **control over contributions and stewardship**, which your product already has; a validated demand construct for **unlistability** does not appear.

## 3. Revealed preference on platforms offering both modes

- **CaringBridge**: its (now-defunct) help center stated the middle tier is the norm — "The Semi-Public Privacy setting is the most common privacy setting on CaringBridge... only visitors who are signed-in to CaringBridge can view" (caringbridgehelp.custhelp.com, retrievable only as a search snippet; the current Salesforce help center still documents Public / Semi-Private (registered users) / Private tiers but no distribution). **Caveats:** no percentages ever published; and CaringBridge is a *pre-death health-crisis* product where medical privacy norms apply — weak transfer to memorials, whose job is announcement. Even so, note the modal choice is *link-shareable and logged-in-visible*, not invite-only.
- **ForeverMissed, MuchLoved, Keeper**: all offer public/invited/private modes; none publishes a distribution. MuchLoved (UK charity) states "You can switch your Tribute to become private or public at anytime" and that only public tributes appear in search — no default disclosed, no stats (muchloved.com privacy page, fetched).
- **Ever Loved**: could not verify the $199.99 one-time price or that privacy/password protection is its paid differentiator this session (pricing pages 403'd); a 2026 third-party comparison (myfarewelling.com) lists Ever Loved as "Free (fundraising fees apply)" with no privacy upsell mentioned. **No platform discloses what fraction of families buys any privacy upsell.** Treat the "$199.99 partly for password protection" supply-side datum as unconfirmed.

## 4. Qualitative signals

Reddit could not be fetched directly (blocked). From the academic record and platform materials, the recurring family complaints are: trolling/spam on public memorials, unwanted strangers, and obituary-scraping scams — i.e., demand for *gatekeeping of contributions and data*, which is separable from demand for *undiscoverability*. No evidence stream showed families complaining that memorials are "too findable" per se.

## 5. Implications for Memorial Pages, with confidence levels

| Claim | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| A large mainstream segment prefers unlisted memorials | None found | Low (unsupported) |
| Middle-tier visibility (findable-by-link, gated contribution) is the modal preference | CaringBridge modal tier; Marwick & Ellison; platform defaults | Moderate |
| Announcement/discovery is a core job the product must not block | Obituary function; NFDA digital-channel adoption (40% found funeral home via Facebook) | Moderate-high |
| Moderation/steward control is the validated demand | Brubaker line of work; trolling literature; all platforms ship it | High |

**Strategic read:** the demand-side record validates your *moderation/steward* architecture strongly, but validates *unlistability as identity* not at all. The evidence pattern (middle-tier modal choice; no platform leading with "unfindable"; NFDA silence) is most consistent with the counter-hypothesis: privacy should be a **spectrum setting with unlisted as one option** (possibly the default), paired with an opt-in announcement layer (shareable public landing card / obituary link) — not the product's whole identity. If a hard number is needed before committing, the only path is primary research: a 200–400-person survey of recently bereaved adults, or instrumenting your own beta's visibility choices.
