# Dedicated Memorial/Tribute-Page Platforms — Competitive Landscape (researched 2026-08-28)

Scope: direct competitors to a privacy-first, unlisted, family-moderated memorial-page product. Data drawn from vendor pricing pages, help centers, Trustpilot/BBB, press releases, and trade press. Pricing verified against primary pages where fetchable; exceptions flagged.

## 1. Legacy.com — the incumbent (obituary aggregator, not really a memorial builder)

- **What/who**: Founded 1998, Chicago; private. The world's largest commercial obituary provider — partner network of 1,500+ newspapers and thousands of funeral homes, hosting obituaries for ~70% of US deaths (~3.5M obituaries/year). 30–44M unique monthly visitors depending on source/year ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy.com), [Legacy advertising page via search](https://www.legacy.com/contact/en/articles/11434101-how-to-advertise-on-legacy)). Channel is overwhelmingly B2B2C (newspapers + funeral homes), not family-direct.
- **Features**: obituary publication, public guest books (nearly 100M entries as of 2016; claims to review >1M guest-book submissions/month), sympathy-flower/gift e-commerce, obituary search/alerts. 2025: launched "MarketIQ" competitive-intelligence product for funeral homes — it is increasingly a data/SaaS business for the trade ([Legacy PR](https://www.legacy.com/professionalsuite/article/legacycom-launches-marketiq-the-first-competitive-intelligence-tool-built-exclusively-for-funeral)).
- **Pricing/monetization**: families pay newspapers to publish (US newspaper obit market ~$500M/yr; Legacy-related placements generated $50M+ for newspapers late 2022–early 2024); Legacy takes a share, plus ad revenue and e-commerce commissions. Guest books historically expire unless "sponsored": $29/year or $79 "in perpetuity" per its [Terms of Use](https://www.legacy.com/terms-of-use) — a long-criticized pattern ("The Great Legacy.com Swindle", [Idle Words, 2010](https://idlewords.com/2010/07/the_great_legacy.com_swindle.htm); [Slate, 2017](https://slate.com/technology/2017/12/legacy-com-has-cornered-the-market-on-death-online.html)).
- **Privacy**: fully public, SEO-indexed, searchable — the exact opposite of unlisted.
- **User sentiment**: poor on consumer review sites ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/legacy.com), [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/il/chicago/profile/obituary-notification-service/legacycom-0654-88160850/complaints)): scammers monitor new obituaries and pose as relatives in guest books, families can't delete scam messages themselves; surprise checkout fees; billing/cancellation complaints.
- **Status**: active, dominant, drifting further B2B. **Relevance**: Legacy's public/searchable model is precisely what creates its scam and grief-vulture problems — validation of the unlisted, steward-moderated model.

## 2. Ever Loved — family-direct freemium + funeral marketplace (US)

- **What/who**: San Francisco, founded 2017 (YC; ~$2M seed incl. Social Capital; founder Alison Johnston, ex-Google/InstaEDU) ([TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/09/ever-loved/), [TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/12/casket-prices-ever-loved/)). Family-direct; also courts funeral homes/hospices as referral partners.
- **Features**: free memorial website + obituary, event pages with RSVP and livestream link, integrated funeral-cost crowdfunding, charity donations, guest book, and a funeral products marketplace (caskets/urns ~50% below mortuary prices).
- **Pricing**: standard sites free forever; Premium is a **one-time $199.99** (24-hr free trial) and is largely a *privacy/de-commercialization* upsell: remove product/gift sections, limit who can post, password-protect ([help center](https://support.everloved.com/article/277-how-much-does-an-ever-loved-memorial-site-cost)). Fundraising: no platform fee, Stripe's 2.9%+30¢ passed through; monetized via optional donor tips and vendor partnerships ([help center](https://support.everloved.com/article/148-are-memorial-websites-and-obituaries-really-free)).
- **Privacy**: public by default; privacy is the paid feature. Visitor registration required to post (per [online-tribute's comparison](https://www.online-tribute.com/memorial-website-guide)).
- **Sentiment**: mixed. Praise for fundraising UX; serious complaints on [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/everloved.com)/[BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/san-francisco/profile/internet-marketing-services/ever-loved-1116-926048/complaints): a false obituary by a non-family member that the platform was slow to act on (anyone can create a memorial for anyone — no verification of relationship to the deceased), memorial takedowns without explanation, disputed donation charges.
- **Status**: active. **Relevance**: proves families will pay one-time ~$200 specifically for privacy and ad/upsell removal; also shows the failure mode of not verifying who "owns" a death.

## 3. GatheringUs — acquired; now Keeper's virtual-funeral services arm

- NYC startup for virtual/hybrid funerals + memorial pages. **Acquired by Keeper Memorials Dec 2022**; all memorial pages migrated to MyKeeper.com; GatheringUs continues only as a high-touch virtual-funeral service brand ([about page](https://events.gatheringus.com/pages/about-us), [PitchBook on Keeper](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/515985-49)).
- **Pricing** (services, not SaaS): Livestream-only $750; Core $1,400–1,650; Premium $2,100; hybrid up to $2,300 — human-produced events with speaker training, edited recordings, reception rooms ([pricing page](https://events.gatheringus.com/pages/virtualfuneralservices)).
- **Relevance**: standalone memorial-page startups struggle to survive independently; the exit was to a consolidator. High-touch event production is a real (but ops-heavy) revenue line — the opposite of near-zero-ops.

## 4. Forever Missed — the veteran family-direct freemium builder

- **What/who**: operating since ~2008; family-direct; self-described as funded by donations and upgrades.
- **Features**: biography, tributes, unlimited photo/video (paid), music playlists, story illustrations, multiple admins, templates (incl. military/children), ad-free.
- **Pricing**: the pricing page ([forevermissed.com/ourplans](https://www.forevermissed.com/ourplans)) blocked automated fetching (HTTP 403), so exact current numbers are unverified first-hand. Third-party 2026 sources converge on roughly **$9.95/month premium, lifetime one-time ~$155–160** ([online-tribute guide](https://www.online-tribute.com/memorial-website-guide): $159.95 lifetime / $9.95 mo; other trackers cite $8.99/mo, $74.99/yr, $154.99 lifetime — prices have clearly risen over time from an earlier $45/yr / $119 lifetime). Free tier limited to ~5 photos.
- **Privacy**: configurable (public / password / invite); but **visitors must register an account to leave tributes** — repeatedly cited as a participation barrier for elderly relatives.
- **Sentiment**: strongest consumer reputation in the category — ~4.7–5★ across 1,000+ [Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/forevermissed.com); minority complaints about account/content deletion after lapsed payments and difficult restoration.
- **Status**: active, stable, dated design. **Relevance**: the closest analog to Memorial Pages' model (family-direct freemium, privacy controls); its two weaknesses — registration friction for contributors and pay-or-lose-content anxiety — are directly addressable.

## 5. Keeper (mykeeper.com) — closest feature-set competitor, incl. QR-to-grave

- **What/who**: founded 2013, Montreal. Dual-channel: family-direct plus B2B for funeral homes, cemeteries and hospices (API/plugin integrations with funeral-home CMS for auto-created memorials; cemetery kiosk product with grave mapping/walking directions; hospice channel) ([mykeeper.com/cemeteries](https://www.mykeeper.com/cemeteries), [for-business](https://www.mykeeper.com/for-business)). Acquired GatheringUs (2022).
- **Features**: memorial pages with guest book/tributes, media gallery, **AI obituary assistant**, family tree, event pages (virtual/in-person/hybrid), content moderation, custom URL, geotagged resting place, **QR-code memorial plaques sold in its shop** ([KeeperQR](https://www.mykeeper.com/shop/collections/qrcode)).
- **Pricing** ([pricing page](https://www.mykeeper.com/pricing)): Free = 1 memorial, up to 5 photos/videos, most features included; **Keeper Plus $99 one-time** = unlimited memorials, unlimited HD media, unlimited admins, downloads; **Concierge $350 one-time** = white-glove setup with 1:1 meetings. Also monetizes flowers/gift-basket e-commerce on pages.
- **Privacy**: spectrum from fully public to fully private, family-only sharing, private-images-on-public-page. No visitor registration required to contribute (per online-tribute comparison).
- **Sentiment**: 5★ / ~233 [Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/mykeeper.com); praised for ease, support, free tier. Criticized for limited customization and on-page commerce.
- **Status**: active, consolidating (GatheringUs). **Relevance**: **the most direct competitor** — one-time-payment model, QR plaques, moderation, concierge tier. Differences: Keeper defaults toward discoverable/public pages and funeral-home distribution; it is not unlisted-by-design.

## 6. MuchLoved (UK) — the charity model

- **What/who**: UK registered charity (No. 1118590), Charitable Trust established 2007 by Jonathan Davies; the dominant UK tribute platform. Channel: ~2,500 funeral-director branches + 500+ charity partners (RNLI, Alzheimer's Society, etc.) creating tribute funds ([about page](https://www.muchloved.com/gateway/about-muchloved/)).
- **Features**: tribute pages with photos/videos/stories, virtual candles and gifts, funeral details, events, anniversaries, and its core engine: **in-memory fundraising** with Gift Aid for any UK charity.
- **Pricing**: pages free forever, no subscriptions; sustained by a small fee on donations. Scale: **500,000+ tributes; £80M+ raised** for 14,000+ UK charities ([muchloved.com](https://www.muchloved.com/), about page).
- **Privacy**: granular access/invitation and display controls; pages can be private.
- **Sentiment**: 4★ / ~47 [Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/muchloved.com) — small volume, generally positive; third-party critiques: dated design, visitor registration, candle upsells, UK-only relevance.
- **Status**: active, entrenched via charity/funeral-director channel. **Relevance**: proves in-memory *charity fundraising* is the strongest sustainable free-model engine in this category — a feature Memorial Pages lacks entirely.

## 7. Memories.net / Memories Group (AU) — the "digital legacy" consolidator

- **What/who**: Australian startup (founded 2014 as Skymorials; rebranded Memories 2020; CEO Tom Ainsworth); raised **A$31M** to build a "wholesome social media platform" around memories ([SmartCompany, 2022](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/startupsmart/memories-31-million-wholesome-social-media-platform/)). Acquired **HeavenAddress** (Dec 2020) and reportedly US-based Afterlife.com in 2021 (claimed $12M / 20M users — single-source, unverified). Claimed 9M+ users across 195 countries (company claim, not audited).
- **Features today** ([memories.net](https://memories.net)): tribute-video builder, AI biography/obituary/eulogy writing, order-of-service booklet builder, digital guest book, online memory books + hardcover printing; funeral-home integration.
- **Pricing**: **$99 one-time** for creation tools + **$12/year per-memorial "hosting and care plan"** — a recurring hosting fee on what families expect to be permanent, which comparison sites flag as a continuity worry. Positioning is private/password-protected, ad-free.
- **Status**: active but in flux — legacy Memories pages were recently placed in **read-only migration to a new system**, and the grand social-network ambition has visibly narrowed to funeral-adjacent tools. **Relevance**: cautionary tale — the best-funded "Facebook for the dead" attempt (A$31M) retreated to selling memory books and per-memorial hosting fees.

## 8. Kudoboard — group-card product with a memorial SKU

- **What/who**: mainstream group greeting-board SaaS (workplace focus) with a dedicated memorial tier. Family/colleague-direct.
- **Features**: link-based collaborative board (no contributor account needed), photos/GIFs/videos, slideshow playback at services, print-to-poster, moderation controls, multiple admins, export.
- **Pricing** ([kudoboard.com/pricing](https://www.kudoboard.com/pricing/)): per-board $6.99 (20 posts) / $9.99 (100 posts + video) / $19.99 (unlimited); **Memorial board $99 one-time, unlimited posts** with proactive moderation, privacy, downloads; business subscriptions $299–449/yr.
- **Privacy**: link-based access; not a permanent memorial archive — it's an event artifact.
- **Status**: active, healthy (its economics come from the workplace market). **Relevance**: shows demand for *frictionless contribution* (no login) and *service-day artifacts* (slideshow, printed poster) — both natural adjacencies for a QR-to-grave product; and that "$99 one-time memorial" is an established price point across Keeper, Kudoboard, and Memories.

## 9. Tribute Archive — the funeral-home-channel obituary archive

- **What/who**: consumer-facing obituary archive of **Tribute Technology**, the PE-built funeral-tech rollup (Providence Equity assembled Frazer Consultants/FrontRunner/SRS from 2018; sold to **Carlyle Group + Vista-backed ownership**) serving 9,000+ funeral homes ([Who Owns Whom](https://outcompetemarketing.com/who-owns-whom/), [tributetech.com](https://www.tributetech.com/tribute-tech)). Pure funeral-home channel; families never choose it directly.
- **Features**: permanent public obituary + "Tribute Wall" (condolences, photos, videos), memorial-tree program (one tree donated per obituary), heavy e-commerce (Tribute Store flowers/sympathy gifts) ([tributearchive.com](https://www.tributearchive.com/)).
- **Pricing**: free to families (funeral home pays for the platform); monetized via flower/gift commissions.
- **Privacy**: public, indexed, searchable.
- **Status**: active; the default "memorial page" most US families actually get, bundled with the funeral home's website. **Relevance**: this is the incumbent experience Memorial Pages competes against for mindshare — generic, ad/commerce-laden, funeral-home-branded, public.

## 10. Afterword — pivoted to funeral-home SaaS

- Founded 2019 (Effie Anolik); began family-facing (virtual memorials, planning), now sells an "AI-first operating system for funeral homes": case management, online arrangement, payments, AI assistant "Grace". **Pricing is per-funeral-home**: Essential $79/mo, Task Lists $99/mo, Chain of Custody $149/mo, Online Planner $179/mo, Personalized Case Management $325/mo ([afterword.com/pricing](https://afterword.com/pricing/), [FuneralVision](https://www.funeralvision.com/afterword-launches-essential-software-for-independent-funeral-homes/)). Families get a secondary portal. **Relevance**: another consumer-memorial founder who concluded the money is in B2B funeral-home software, not family-direct memorials.

## 11. HeavenAddress — legacy AU/NZ/Asia memorial network, absorbed

- Founded 2009, Sydney; funeral-home-channel memorial/death-notice site; acquired by Memories Group Dec 2020, being folded into the Memories app/platform ([HeavenAddress Medium](https://heavenaddress.medium.com/heaven-address-is-an-established-online-memorial-community-where-you-can-commemorate-and-honour-64aaf6baf552)). Notices cost **AUD $180** direct, less via partnered funeral homes ([Family Funeral Services blog](https://www.familyfuneralservices.com.au/blog/online-memorials/discover-heavenaddress-online-memorials/)). Public/searchable. Status: alive as a brand, effectively sunsetting into Memories.

## 12. Chptr — video memorials; pivoted from social app to broadcast infrastructure

- **What/who**: founded 2022 by Rehan Choudhry; consumer app for crowdsourced video memories ("YouTube for memorialization"; ~6,000 users / 3,000+ videos as of early trade coverage) ([FuneralVision](https://www.funeralvision.com/has-rehan-choudhry-created-the-youtube-for-memorialization/)).
- **Pivot**: now "infrastructure for hyperlocal media" — converts funeral-home inputs into ready-to-air TV/radio memorial segments in <24h; partnership with Tribute Technology (9,000+ funeral homes) and iHeartMedia; live across 132 US TV markets; **$5.5M Series A, June 2026** (CityRock) ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/chptr-raises-5-5m-series-a-to-build-the-infrastructure-for-timely-hyperlocal-media-distribution-across-tv-radio-and-digital-302798241.html), [Tribute Tech PR, Aug 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tribute-technology-and-chptr-launch-nations-largest-broadcast-memorial-platform-across-us-tv-markets-302534627.html)).
- **Pricing**: $99 service listing; $250 pro 1-min video; $850 1-min broadcast; $2,500 3-min broadcast; Chptr keeps 12.5%, rest split with funeral homes/TV partners.
- **Relevance**: the highest-profile recent consumer memorial *social app* found consumer social-graph growth insufficient and pivoted to B2B distribution. Strong evidence against the "richer social memorial network" thesis.

## 13. Other notable entrants and exits

- **Farewelling** ([myfarewelling.com](https://www.myfarewelling.com/)): design-forward family-direct memorials + funeral-planning toolkit; free starter, ~$60/yr or $149 lifetime.
- **Online-Tribute, GetMemorial, After.io, Murial, Afterlife.ai, CreateMemorial** — a 2024–26 swarm of small AI-flavored memorial builders (AI obituary writers, timelines, $80–$100 lifetime price points, SEO comparison-content marketing). Low moats, unclear longevity ([online-tribute guide](https://www.online-tribute.com/memorial-website-guide), [after.io](https://after.io/), [GetMemorial](https://www.getmemorial.com/blog/best-online-memorial-websites)).
- **LifeWeb 360** (memorial scrapbooks): acquired by HistoryIT late 2025, relaunched as org-focused "MemoryPort"; **no longer offered free to individuals** ([lifeweb360.com](https://www.lifeweb360.com/)).
- **AI "grief-tech" adjacency**: StoryFile filed Chapter 11 in 2024 ([AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/verticals/startup-behind-ai-william-shatner-files-for-bankruptcy)), emerged March 2025 under new ownership ([AV Magazine](https://www.avinteractive.com/territories-news/us-canada/conversational-ai-video-pioneer-emerges-from-chapter-11-03-03-2025/)); **HereAfter AI is shutting down** (notice live 2026) ([Afterlife.ai report](https://www.afterlife.ai/hereafter-ai-shutting-down)). Deadbot/avatar products face consent backlash. The durable AI use in this category is mundane: obituary/eulogy drafting assistance (Keeper, Memories, Afterword, After.io all ship it).
- **Historical churn**: multiple free memorial services (Respectance, Afterlife.com, older free hosts) have closed or been absorbed with little notice — comparison sites explicitly warn families about platform mortality; "what happens if the company dies" is a live consumer anxiety.

## What nobody in this list does well

1. **Genuinely unlisted, privacy-first memorials as the default.** Every major player is either fully public/SEO-driven (Legacy, Tribute Archive, HeavenAddress, Ever Loved's free tier) or treats privacy as a toggle/upsell (Ever Loved Premium, Keeper, Forever Missed). None is architected around unindexable, unenumerable pages. Legacy's guest-book scam problem is the direct cost of the public model.
2. **Contributor friction vs. safety — no one solves both.** Forever Missed and MuchLoved require visitor registration (kills participation from elderly relatives); Kudoboard and Keeper drop the login but rely on thin moderation. Email-verified contributors + automated tri-tier screening + steward approval is a combination none of them ship.
3. **Trustworthy permanence.** The category's history is acquisitions, migrations, read-only freezes, hosting fees on "forever" pages (Memories' $12/yr), and pay-or-expire guest books (Legacy). No platform offers a credible, published permanence/portability guarantee (data export, endowment-style prepaid hosting, dead-steward succession). The 90-day inactivity failsafe idea is unique.
4. **QR-to-grave integrated with a private page.** Keeper sells QR plaques, but pointing at broadly public pages; cemeteries get kiosks, not families. QR plaque → unlisted, moderated page is an unoccupied niche.
5. **Family-direct economics without necro-commerce.** Almost everyone monetizes grief-adjacent commerce on the memorial itself (flowers, gifts, candles, trees, tips) — the top consumer complaint theme. Clean subscription economics with zero on-page commerce is a differentiator only Forever Missed approximates.
6. **In-memory charity fundraising outside the UK.** MuchLoved shows it's the strongest engine in the category (£80M raised, funeral-director lock-in); no US privacy-respecting equivalent exists at quality.
7. **"Social network for the dead" — repeatedly attempted, never sustained.** The evidence is one-directional: Memories raised A$31M for a wholesome memory social network and retreated to funeral tools + hosting fees; Chptr pivoted from consumer social app to B2B broadcast infrastructure; Afterword pivoted to funeral-home SaaS; GatheringUs and LifeWeb 360 sold; HereAfter AI shut down. Sustained businesses in this space are either (a) funeral-home-channel B2B, (b) charity-fundraising rails, or (c) modest family-direct freemium with one-time ~$99–$200 payments. Death is episodic, audiences per page are small and time-decaying, and there is no viral loop — the structural reason "Facebook for the dead" keeps failing.
