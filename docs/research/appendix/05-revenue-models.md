## 2. Model-by-model analysis

### 2a. Recurring subscription ("rent on grief")
ForeverMissed is the main pure-subscription player and it works commercially (1,100+ mostly-positive Trustpilot reviews), but the model has a structural psychology problem documented since 2010: Legacy.com's **$29/year guest-book renewal emails** — "your loved one's guest book will expire" — became the canonical case study in grief-monetization backlash ([Idle Words](https://idlewords.com/2010/07/the_great_legacy.com_swindle.htm) documented the emails, the undisclosed pricing at checkout, and the "galling" margin on $79-in-perpetuity). Legacy has since retreated: guestbooks on its obituaries are now free/attached by default ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy.com)), and its revenue shifted to placement fees + e-commerce.

Economics: no memorial-specific churn data is published anywhere I could find (stated explicitly: **unverified**). General consumer-subscription benchmarks: **~4–7% monthly churn for B2C digital subscriptions; 5–10%/mo typical for consumer brands** ([mean.ceo benchmark roundup](https://blog.mean.ceo/subscription-churn-retention-benchmarks-statistics/), [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-subscription-churn-rate-by-category)). Memorials plausibly churn *worse than average on the payer side* — the steward's engagement peaks at death and decays, and cancelling feels like a second bereavement decision (which cuts both ways: guilt suppresses cancellation short-term, then produces angry "they held my mother's memorial hostage" reviews — exactly the auto-renewal complaints visible in ForeverMissed's review tail). Annual billing tied to a "remembrance day" cadence mitigates but doesn't remove the "rent on grief" framing.

### 2b. One-time "lifetime/forever" fee
The market has converged here: Keeper ($99), Ever Loved ($199.99), Kudoboard ($99), ForeverMissed lifetime (~$155–160), Turning Hearts (bundled in hardware). It matches the buyer's mental model — a memorial is a *monument*, purchased once — and matches how funerals are bought (one large emotionally-charged basket, price-insensitive at the margin; a $99–$350 line item is invisible next to a $7,000–$10,000 US funeral).

The economics problem is perpetual-hosting liability. Precedents of broken permanence: **1000Memories** (YC, Greylock, $2.5M raised) was acquired by Ancestry in 2012 and its memorial archives were shut down in late 2013 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/10/03/ancestry-com-acquires-photo-digitization-and-sharing-service-1000memories/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000Memories)); memorial-comparison sites note multiple 2015–2020 free platforms whose tributes vanished ([Scan2Remember guide](https://scan2remember.com/blogs/memorial-guides/best-online-memorial-websites-2025) — conflicted source, but the 1000Memories case is independently verified). Newspapers themselves broke the promise in the other direction — putting old obits behind paywalls and charging families ~$79.99 to re-expose them ([Slate 2017](https://slate.com/technology/2017/12/legacy-com-has-cornered-the-market-on-death-online.html)).

The only rigorously funded "forever" model found: **Permanent Legacy Foundation** (permanent.org), a 501(c)(3) charging a **one-time $10/GB** that goes into an endowment sized (per David Rosenthal's storage-economics work) so investment returns cover perpetual storage ([Long Now write-up](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-permanent-legacy-foundation-wants-to-preserve-your-digital-legacy-for-future-generations/)). Cemetery perpetual-care funds are the offline analogue. Practical translation for a small product: price the lifetime tier at a multiple of NPV of hosting (text+compressed images are cents/GB-month; a $99–150 fee over-covers realistic media budgets many times over), escrow a stated fraction, and publish a data-exit covenant (static-export guarantee if the company dies). Turning Hearts' "hosted forever, no fees ever" on a $79.99 hardware sale is the model's unfunded extreme — nothing visible backs the promise.

### 2c. Freemium upsell
Benchmarks: consumer self-serve freemium converts **~2–5% free→paid** (consumer/e-commerce apps cluster near 2%; best-in-class 6–8%+) ([Artisan Strategies benchmark](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks), [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium/)). Memorial freemium has an unusual twist: the *paywall placement* is morally loaded. Ever Loved gates dignity controls (privacy, removing commerce links, restricting posters) — which reads poorly for a privacy-first product ("pay us or your mother's page shows shopping links"). Keeper gates capacity (5 photos free) — cleaner, but a 5-photo memorial feels punitive mid-grief. ForeverMissed gates media volume and admin count. For the product in question, the existing split (free: 1 page; paid: multiple pages, custom slug, plaque PDFs, co-stewards) is unusually defensible because *safety and dignity are never behind the paywall* — only capacity and artifacts. Expect conversion at or below 2–5% if payment is optional, materially higher if the plaque PDF (a physical-world artifact) is the trigger, since it converts at a moment of active intent.

### 2d. B2B via the funeral trade
How the channel actually works: funeral-home website/software vendors (Tribute Technology, Tukios, CFS, Gather, Passare) charge the *home* little or nothing for web presence and recoup via e-commerce on obituary pages — flowers, memorial trees, keepsakes — with **commission profit-sharing back to the funeral home** ([Tribute Technology](https://www.tributetech.com/tribute-store) reports funeral-home e-commerce commissions up 7% H1-2025 [in its own PR](https://www.tributetech.com/tribute-technology-announces-strong-start-to-2025-driven-by-innovation-and-digital-reach); [CFS](https://www.runcfs.com/) is explicit: free websites, "we share our profits with you" on sympathy-flower commissions; Tukios websites $0, paid modules $99/mo). Legacy.com's newspaper variant: it runs 1,500+ papers' obit sections and its consumer placement flow delivered **110,000 obits and $52M to newspapers from late 2022 to early 2024** ([NNA](https://www.nna.org/legacys-consumer-direct-placement-delivers-110k-obits-and-52m-in-revenue-to-newspapers)); US newspapers overall take ~$500M/yr in paid obituaries ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy.com)). MuchLoved shows the acquisition power of the channel even without money changing hands: **~70% of its £29M/yr donation volume originates on funeral-director-created pages**. Keeper's B2B arm (white-label memorial pages + QR, API-linked to cemetery/funeral management systems) is the closest comp to selling *this* product through the trade. The catch: the channel expects either rev-share on commerce or a per-case/white-label fee, and funeral homes want their brand on the page — in tension with an unlisted, family-controlled, commerce-light product. Monument dealers/engravers (Turning Hearts' actual channel, at ~50% wholesale margins on medallions) fit a QR-plaque product far more naturally than funeral homes do.

### 2e. Affiliate / marketplace commerce
Legacy.com is the proof: e-commerce (mostly flowers, sold on-page with local-florist fulfillment) grew **40%+/yr for five straight years and now equals its entire ad revenue** ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/legacy-com-turning-obits-business/)). Ever Loved runs ~10% margins on caskets/urns ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/12/casket-prices-ever-loved/)). Key structural insight: the *payer* is the sympathy-gift-buying **visitor**, not the grieving family — which neutralizes most of the exploitation optics. But it depends on traffic volume and purchase-intent moments (obituary week), and an unlisted, unindexable page with dozens (not thousands) of visitors has weak affiliate economics. Memorial trees ($9.95–$42, huge margin) are the one commerce item that stays on-brand for a quiet memorial page.

### 2f. Fundraising fees
GoFundMe (0% platform, tips + 2.9%+$0.30) and Ever Loved (same) have set the consumer expectation at **zero platform fee** for memorial fundraising; MuchLoved's charity-run 3.2% is the ceiling and it's a nonprofit. A for-profit percentage on funeral donations is now reputationally radioactive (see Legacy.com's Givealike scandal — a plugin that diverted designated charity donations and skimmed fees, settled with Vermont's AG, per [Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2017/12/legacy-com-has-cornered-the-market-on-death-online.html)). Tips-only fundraising is viable as an engagement/retention feature, not as a primary revenue line at small scale (GoFundMe's $330M/yr memorial volume × tip rates works only at massive volume).

### 2g. Print/physical products (plaques, books, keepsakes)
This is where grief spending is *expected*: headstones cost $1,000–$10,000+, and buyers already accept physical permanence pricing. QR plaques retail $50–$100 (Turning Hearts $79.99–$99.99; Scan2Remember $49.90), memorial keepsake books (photo-book printing) carry 50–70% gross margins at $40–120 price points, and the range extends to $3,499+ diamonds. Physical goods are one-time, guilt-free (they're *gifts to the deceased*), and — critically for this product — the QR plaque is not an upsell bolted on but the product's core distribution loop (plaque → grave visitor → new contributor).

### 2h. Ads — why they backfire
Three independent failure modes, all documented: (1) **User revulsion** — Legacy.com's own CMO conceded "advertising is often unwelcome" on obituaries and deliberately pivoted to commerce instead ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/legacy-com-turning-obits-business/)); the 2010 Idle Words piece and years of complaints made grief-adjacent upsell/ads the industry's cautionary tale. (2) **Brand-safety demonetization** — advertisers actively block death content: an Adalytics study found **77% of The Economist's obituaries flagged "unsafe"** by Oracle's brand-safety tech, and "death"/"obituary" are standard keyword blocklist entries, so flagged pages get only bottom-tier CPMs or none ([Brand Safety Institute](https://www.brandsafetyinstitute.com/blog/obituary-pirates-brand-safety), [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2021/11/08/how-spammy-sites-that-rip-off-obituaries-suck-up-ad-dollars-from-major-brands)). The only ad-funded death content that scales is obituary-piracy spam. (3) For an **unlisted, noindex** product specifically, ad inventory is tiny by design — ads would sacrifice the entire privacy positioning for negligible revenue.

## 3. Key company numbers found

- **Legacy.com:** CEO ballparked revenue "between $20M and $100M" (2016, [Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2017/12/legacy-com-has-cornered-the-market-on-death-online.html)); 40M monthly uniques, 20M+ obits; third-party estimates range wildly ($7.9M LeadIQ to $98.7M Growjo — both low-confidence); >1M guestbook entries human-moderated monthly, incl. proactive "mistress screening" ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/looking-mistresses-obit-site-legacy-keeps-trolls-bay/)) — evidence that moderation is the real cost center of memorial platforms, which this product's three-tier automated + steward model addresses at near-zero marginal cost.
- **Ever Loved:** YC S19, only ~$150K–seed-scale funding disclosed ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/ever-loved/__gzYO1L5LJtzkan0ArJOT68vA0laSJ_8Q5rnTRwOTIBw), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/09/ever-loved/)); revenue "up nearly 20X YoY" in Feb 2020 (from a tiny base). Runs lean — a proof that this category supports small-team economics, not venture blitzscale.
- **MuchLoved 2024:** £29M donations/yr, 3.2% fee → ~£0.9M/yr processing income as a charity; 15,000 charity partners.
- **GoFundMe memorial category:** 125K campaigns/yr, $330M/yr, ~$2,640 average raise; only ~27% of campaigns hit goal.

## 4. Most defensible revenue mixes for a privacy-first, unlisted, steward-moderated memorial product

**Mix A — "Monument pricing": one-time lifetime fee as the core, capacity-freemium on top.** Free: 1 page, safety/privacy never gated. Paid: one-time $99–$179 per memorial (market-validated band: Keeper $99, Kudoboard $99, ForeverMissed ~$155, Ever Loved $199.99) unlocking custom slug, co-stewards, media headroom. Evidence: every scaled consumer memorial player now leads with one-time pricing; subscription is the documented backlash generator (Legacy guest books). Fund the "forever" claim credibly — publish a hosting endowment ratio (permanent.org precedent) and a static-export/dead-company covenant; this converts the sector's biggest broken promise (1000Memories et al.) into a trust differentiator that privacy-first buyers will pay a premium for. Keep the existing Stripe subscription only as an optional "family plan" for multi-page power users (genealogy-adjacent buyers), where recurring billing reads as an account fee, not rent on one person's grave.

**Mix B — Physical-artifact attach: QR plaques and keepsake print as the profit engine.** The plaque PDF is already built; the upsell is fulfilled hardware ($59–$99 engraved medallion, ~50%+ margin at Turning Hearts' own wholesale economics) and an annual printed memory book compiled from approved contributions ($49–$99, photo-book COGS ~30–50%). Evidence: grief spending concentrates in physical permanence; visitors and extended family can be buyers (gift dynamic, no "rent" framing); Turning Hearts built an entire company on this single SKU. This mix uniquely reinforces the product's QR-to-grave loop instead of fighting the unlisted design.

**Mix C — Trade channel via monument dealers and cemeteries (not funeral-home ad-commerce).** White-label/bundled memorial pages + medallions sold wholesale to engravers, monument companies, and cemeteries (Keeper's B2B playbook; Turning Hearts' reseller network; MuchLoved's 70%-of-volume-via-funeral-directors proof that the trade originates most memorial pages). Price per-case ($20–50 wholesale per memorial+plaque bundle) rather than rev-share on flowers — the flower-commission machine (Tribute Tech/CFS/Legacy) is the incumbent model and is incompatible with an unlisted, commerce-clean page. This channel buys distribution at the exact moment of need without consumer ad spend, and monument dealers have no conflicting software incumbent the way funeral homes do.

**Mix D (adjunct, not core) — zero-fee, tips-optional memorial fundraising + memorial-tree gifting.** Matches the GoFundMe/Ever Loved consumer expectation (0% platform fee), adds a reason for the page to circulate in week one, and memorial-tree gifting ($10–$40, high margin, visitor-paid) is the one commerce item that doesn't degrade a dignified page. Evidence: MuchLoved shows donations are the strongest engagement driver on tribute pages; but at unlisted-product scale this is engagement glue and single-digit-% revenue, not a business.

**Explicitly avoid:** advertising in any form (triple-documented failure: user revulsion per Legacy's own CMO, brand-safety demonetization of death content, and near-zero inventory on unlisted pages); percentage fees on donations (Givealike scandal; 0% is the consumer standard); and unfunded "forever" promises (1000Memories precedent) — if the word "forever" is used, publish what funds it.
