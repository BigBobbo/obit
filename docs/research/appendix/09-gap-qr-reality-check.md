# GAP: QR-to-grave wedge: is there ANY evidence people scan grave QR codes, and does rising cremation undercut the anchor?

# Grave-QR Reality Check: Scan Data, Vendor Traction, Cemetery Permission, Cremation Drift

## 1. Empirical scan-rate data: none exists publicly

After ~25 source fetches across vendor sites, vendor blogs, cemetery program pages, QR-analytics marketing, trade press, and search sweeps for academic work: **no vendor, cemetery, or study publishes a single grave-QR scan statistic** (scans per plaque, scan-to-contribution conversion, or visitor counts).

- Turning Hearts ([turninghearts.com](https://turninghearts.com)): no scan/user counts anywhere on site; only "Rated 4.8 out of 5 stars / 140 Reviews" (on-site).
- Scan2Remember ([homepage](https://scan2remember.com)) and its SEO guide library: zero quantitative usage data; only testimonial anecdotes ("Every time we visit the cemetery, we scan it and relive those memories").
- Keeper ([mykeeper.com](https://www.mykeeper.com)): "Millions of Memories Shared" — no verifiable numbers, no KeeperQR stats.
- Bitly's [headstone-QR blog post](https://bitly.com/blog/qr-codes-for-headstones/) — the obvious place for a scan-analytics case study — is purely promotional; its only external case study is an airport, not a memorial.
- Cemetery-run program: Mt. Elliott Cemetery Association (Detroit) sells a $99 one-time QR memorial via partner "Memory Links," staff-installed ([mtelliott.com/memories](https://www.mtelliott.com/memories/)) — again no adoption or scan metrics.
- Quiring Monuments "Living Headstone" (since 2011): [monuments.com](https://www.monuments.com/living-headstones) returned HTTP 403; no usage data retrievable. Notably, [Death Reference Desk noted in 2012](https://www.deathreferencedesk.org/2012/07/15/qr-codes-on-gravestones-second-verse-same-as-the-first/) that grave-QR press coverage was *already* a recycled hype story then — 14 years of coverage, still no published usage data.
- Negative anecdote: funeral.com's guide references a 2025 incident of 1,000+ unauthorized QR stickers placed on Munich graves ([funeral.com article](https://www.funeral.com/blogs/the-journal/qr-medallions-on-graves-how-digital-links-work-privacy-risks-and-cemetery-approval)) — QR-on-grave activity making news as vandalism, not engagement.

**Verdict: the "core distribution loop" claim rests on zero public evidence of scan behavior.** The loop is unproven, not disproven — but 15 years post-Living-Headstone, absence of any vendor bragging about scans is itself informative.

## 2. Turning Hearts: a real but very small business

- Founded 2020, Lindon, Utah; **4 employees** (Tracxn, as of Jul 2024: [profile](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/turninghearts/__PKpuUEgGbcT3KYX98mKUeyzDyOCvPfN596R_nmfzi-s)); **estimated revenue ~$427,775/yr, never raised funding** ([Prospeo estimate](https://prospeo.io/c/turning-hearts-revenue)) — i.e., roughly 5,000 $79.99 two-pack units/yr if all revenue were medallions.
- Site: $79.99 two-pack, "No Monthly Fees," lifetime hosting — plus a donation widget ($1–$1,000), suggesting the free-hosting-forever model needs subsidy ([turninghearts.com](https://turninghearts.com)).
- App traction is tiny: Android app "500+ downloads," 17 reviews, 3.7 stars ([Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.qualtryteam.turninghearts)); iOS app released Nov 2024 with **6 ratings** ([iTunes Search API](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=turning+hearts&entity=software&country=US)). No Trustpilot page exists (404).
- Note: Turning Hearts runs a public "Discover" browse feature — the opposite of Memorial Pages' unlisted model.

**Verdict: closest comp is a ~$400K/yr, 4-person hardware-plus-free-hosting seller — a viable niche product, not evidence of a scalable acquisition loop.**

## 3. Cemetery permission: variable, approval-gated; my planned 10-document survey mostly failed

Direct fetches of individual cemetery regulation documents largely failed (Green-Wood's [rules PDF](https://www.green-wood.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/G-W_RulesRegulations.pdf) downloaded but text-extraction failed; Spring Grove, Forest Lawn 404s; Arlington and VA/NCA pages did not expose attachment-policy text). **I could not complete a first-hand survey of 10+ regulation documents; treat cemetery-rule prevalence as unverified.** What the vendor/industry guides confirm:

- funeral.com: "Cemeteries are, by design, rule-driven places" — get written rules and confirm whether "a small QR medallion is treated like a plaque, a decoration, or a monument modification" ([source](https://www.funeral.com/blogs/the-journal/qr-medallions-on-graves-how-digital-links-work-privacy-risks-and-cemetery-approval)).
- Evaheld: "confirm the cemetery's rules, the stonemason's requirements and the family's privacy preferences before anything is engraved or attached"; rules may cover "size, placement, materials, wording, adhesives, attachments" ([source](https://evaheldmemorials.com/blog/put-a-qr-code-on-a-gravestone)).
- Counter-dynamic: some cemeteries now sell their *own* QR program (Mt. Elliott, above) — meaning the graveyard owner can capture this revenue and exclude third-party plaques.

## 4. Cremation shrinks the addressable grave base — and dispositions increasingly lack a fixed site

- NFDA: **63.4% cremation vs 31.6% burial in 2025; 82.3% cremation projected by 2045** ([NFDA statistics](https://nfda.org/news/statistics)). CANA: US 62.8% (2025), 69.1% by 2030 ([CANA statistics](https://www.cremationassociation.org/page/IndustryStatistics); detailed data member-gated).
- Fixed-placement share: CANA/Harris Poll 2022 research found **~26% of US households keep at least one set of cremated remains at home** ([ConnectingDirectors](https://connectingdirectors.com/64074-cana-takeaways); study background [here](https://connectingdirectors.com/63829-cremation-statistics)); CANA-attributed reporting says **"as few as 20% of all cremations"** are interred in a cemetery ([Lookout Santa Cruz](https://lookout.co/graveyards-fade-into-history-how-can-we-pay-our-respects/story)); CANA itself: "over two million sets of cremated remains are abandoned" ([CANA](https://www.cremationassociation.org/cremationoptions.html)). A precise buried/niche/scattered split is not publicly published by NFDA/CANA — flag as a data gap.
- Vendors already hedge to non-grave anchors: Scan2Remember explicitly markets "Mount it on a headstone, urn, bench, or at home."

## Implication for Memorial Pages

Arithmetic: at 2045 rates, only ~18% of deaths produce a casket burial, and perhaps ~20% of the cremated majority get any cemetery placement — so the plaque-addressable share of deaths trends toward roughly a third and falling. Combined with (a) zero public evidence of graveside scan behavior, (b) the best-known vendor being a 4-person, sub-$500K business after 5+ years, and (c) permission friction plus cemeteries competing for the same $99, the QR plaque is best treated as a **paid-tier feature and emotional differentiator, not the growth loop**. If the physical anchor matters strategically, extend it to urns, benches, keepsakes, and in-home objects — and instrument your own plaques (per-plaque scan counts) to generate the usage data nobody in this industry has published.
